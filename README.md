# Renewable Energy Adoption, Electricity Generation and Consumption

Data Analytics mini project that compares renewable energy adoption, electricity generation and electricity consumption across regions and over time (2000-2022).

> **Important:** the main dataset used here (`renewable_energy_electricity_consumption_dataset.csv`) is **synthetic**. It was generated by a script to practise data cleaning and EDA. Only the Ember file contains real data. Results from the synthetic data are illustrative and must not be reported as real-world findings.

## Problem statement

Compare renewable energy adoption, electricity generation and consumption across regions or over time.

## Objectives

1. Collect data on electricity generation, installed capacity, consumption and energy mix.
2. Standardize units, geographical labels and reporting periods.
3. Investigate missing observations, duplicates and inconsistent measurements.
4. Analyze energy mix, growth rates and regional differences.
5. Create stacked bar charts, time-series plots and comparative visualizations.
6. Summarize major trends and identify limitations in the available records.

## Datasets

| Dataset | File | Type | Rows x Columns | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dataset 1 | `renewable_energy_electricity_consumption_dataset.csv` | Real: OWID Dataset | 10,557 x 20 | Generation, installed capacity, energy mix, consumption, population, GDP by country, year and energy source |
| Dataset 2 | `release_generation_yearly_global.csv` | Real: Ember yearly electricity data | 68,222 x 24 | Benchmark for national electricity demand and power-sector emissions |

**Dataset 1 coverage:** 50 countries, 2000-2022, 9 energy sources (hydro, solar, wind, bioenergy, geothermal, coal, gas, oil, nuclear). Messiness was added on purpose: missing values, exact and near-duplicate rows, mixed units (MW/GW, GWh/TWh, TWh/GWh/PJ), inconsistent country, region and source labels, and mixed reporting-period formats (including fiscal years).

**Dataset 2 source:** Ember, Yearly Electricity Data (ember-energy.org). Please check Ember's licence terms and credit Ember when using it.

## Workflow

1. **Setup and loading:** load both CSV files and keep raw copies.
2. **Inspection:** shape, data types, missing values and duplicates for Dataset 1.
3. **Cleaning Dataset 1**
   - Remove 155 exact duplicate rows (10,557 to 10,402).
   - Extract a calendar `year` from mixed `reporting_period` formats; keep 2000-2023.
   - Map energy-source spelling variants to nine standard sources (`clean_source`) and classify them as Renewable or Non Renewable (`clean_source_type`).
   - Map country-name variants to one standard name per country.
   - Standardize units: capacity to GW (`capacity_gw`), generation to TWh (`generation_twh`), consumption to TWh (`consumption_twh`, with PJ converted at 0.277778 TWh per PJ).
   - Set negative values to missing.
4. **Cleaning Dataset 2 (Ember)**
   - Keep country-level rows only (`Area type == "Country or economy"`) to avoid double counting regional aggregates.
   - Keep 2000-2022 and coerce generation, capacity and emissions fields to numeric.
   - Build a benchmark table: `ember_demand_twh` (from `Demand` rows), plus `ember_emissions_mtco2` and `ember_emissions_intensity` (from `Total generation` rows).
5. **Integration:** left-merge Dataset 1 with the Ember benchmark on `iso_code` and `year` to create `df_master`.
6. **Feature engineering**
   - `gdp_per_capita_usd`: GDP divided by population.
   - `clean_share_calculated_%`: a row's generation as a percentage of its country-year total (this is the share of that *source*, not of clean energy).
7. **Analysis and visualisation:** yearly renewable share and year-over-year growth, followed by six charts.

## Preliminary observations

These are the observations written in the notebook summary. They are **preliminary** because Dataset 1 is synthetic and the issues listed below have not been fully resolved.

- **Solar and wind growth:** solar and wind show the fastest growth, accelerating after about 2010.
- **Emissions link:** higher renewable generation is associated with lower grid carbon intensity in the merged Ember metrics.
- **Regional gaps:** renewable adoption differs across regions, with emerging markets relying more on thermal assets.

## Limitations and known issues

**Documented limitations of the data**
- **Synthetic primary dataset:** the numbers are modelled, not measured.
- **Mixed units:** capacity, generation and consumption came in several units, so conversions were needed.
- **Fiscal vs calendar periods:** fiscal-year labels (e.g. `2000-01`) are mapped to their starting calendar year, which creates a small timing mismatch against calendar-year benchmarks.
- **Aggregated Ember rows:** Ember reports broad aggregates (e.g. `Clean`, `Fossil`) alongside individual sources; country-level filtering is used to avoid double counting.

**Issues in the current notebook (to fix before drawing conclusions)**
- **Missing units:** rows with no unit are left unconverted, which inflates some values (e.g. maximum capacity of about 138,453 GW).
- **Outliers:** only negative values are handled; extreme positive values (such as values 10x-100x too large) remain and distort yearly totals and renewable shares.
- **Text values lost:** numbers stored as text (e.g. `1,234.5`, `45.2 MW`) become missing instead of being parsed.
- **Missing text values:** converting columns with `astype(str)` turns missing country, source and ISO values into the strings `nan`/`NAN`. The quarantine step therefore finds 0 rows, and rows with an ISO code of `NAN` cannot be matched with Ember.
- **Region labels:** regions are not standardized, so Chart 3 splits label variants into separate bars.
- **Near-duplicates:** only exact duplicates are removed.
- **Unclassified sources:** rows with a missing source are labelled `Non Renewable` by default.
